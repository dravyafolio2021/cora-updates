# Verified Research Notes - Agency Client Onboarding Playbook

Use these sources only where they strengthen a concrete operating recommendation. Do not turn the guide into an academic paper.

## 1. Scope baseline and change control

**Project Management Institute - Scope Management**
https://www.pmi.org/learning/library/scope-management-9099

Useful principle:
- establish and approve a clear scope baseline before using it to evaluate later changes;
- a request that affects scope, timeline, cost or responsibility should be assessed and authorized before the changed work begins.

Editorial use:
- Chapter 2, when explaining why agencies should make the baseline visible and classify changes before execution.

**Project Management Institute - Scope change control**
https://www.pmi.org/learning/library/scope-control-projects-you-6972

Useful principle:
- identify, define, communicate and authorize meaningful changes instead of letting changed work silently enter delivery.

Editorial use:
- Chapter 2, change-request flow.

## 2. Requirements / context transfer

**Project Management Institute - Effective requirements management**
https://www.pmi.org/learning/library/effective-requirements-management-project-success-8181

Useful principle:
- requirements should be elicited, documented, validated and kept visible enough to reduce rework and ambiguity.

Editorial use:
- Chapters 1 and 3, sales-to-delivery context transfer and information intake.

## 3. Kickoff alignment

**Atlassian Team Playbook - Project Kickoff**
https://www.atlassian.com/team-playbook/plays/project-kickoff

Useful principles:
- kickoff is used to align purpose, roles, responsibilities and success markers;
- not every stakeholder needs every part of the meeting;
- define next steps and owners before the meeting ends.

Editorial use:
- Chapter 5, kickoff agenda and meeting design.

**Atlassian - Nailing your project kick-off**
https://www.atlassian.com/work-management/project-management/project-kickoff/

Useful principles:
- kickoff should cover purpose, scope, communication plan and immediate next steps;
- send a summary and action items afterward.

Editorial use:
- Chapter 5 and Chapter 6.

**Asana - Client Onboarding Process Template**
https://asana.com/templates/client-onboarding-process

Useful principles:
- align internal roles before the client-facing onboarding begins;
- welcome materials, intake, legal/billing tasks and implementation checkpoints work better as a reusable system.

Editorial use:
- Chapter 1 internal handoff and Chapter 7 first 30 days.

## 4. Access and security

**NIST CSRC - Least Privilege**
https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/least_privilege

Useful principle:
- users should receive only the minimum access needed to perform assigned tasks.

Editorial use:
- Chapter 4. Recommend delegated/role-based access instead of collecting primary credentials where platforms support it.

**NIST - Role-Based Access Control**
https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/role_based_access_control

Useful principle:
- access can be assigned by role and responsibility rather than by giving broad individual privileges.

Editorial use:
- Chapter 4 examples for Shopify collaborator access, Meta Business roles, Google Ads manager access, etc.

## Editorial guardrail

Do not use:
- invented retention percentages;
- invented onboarding time benchmarks;
- unsupported claims that a particular number of hours or days guarantees better retention;
- claims such as 'verified operating system' unless there is actual validation data;
- broad legal/security claims beyond what the cited source supports.

The guide should feel authoritative because the operating logic is clear, not because it is stuffed with statistics.
